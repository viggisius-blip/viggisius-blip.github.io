✨ What Is omnarr?

omnarr is a single front end — a single starting screen — for everything stored on your self-hosted media server. If you run a home server and keep books, audiobooks, films, TV shows, comics and games on it, omnarr brings all of those separate collections together behind one clean, easy-to-use search and browsing experience.

Think of it as the front door to your media library. You open omnarr, type in what you are looking for, and it finds it across the different services you already run. When you want something you do not own yet, you can request it from the same screen. And when you are ready to enjoy something, you can start playing it right away without opening a different app for every type of media.

omnarr is designed for homelab users and self-hosters who love having their own media at home but want less clicking and less switching between browser tabs.

🎯 What You Can Do With omnarr

omnarr is built around four everyday actions, and all of them live in the same place:

  • Search. Type a title once and look across every collection you have connected, instead of searching each service separately.
  • Browse. Explore your books, audiobooks, films, shows, comics and games in a clean layout that is easy to scan.
  • Request. Ask for something you do not have yet and let your existing setup handle the rest.
  • Play. Start listening to or watching what you found without leaving omnarr.

🔌 Works With the Services You Already Run

omnarr does not replace your media server. It sits on top of it and connects to the tools you already use, so nothing has to be moved or rebuilt. The services it is designed to work alongside include:

Calibre Audiobookshelf Jellyfin Sonarr Radarr Komga RomM

You can connect only the services you actually use. There is no need to set up anything you do not already have running at home.

🖥️ Before You Begin

omnarr is a companion for a self-hosted media setup, so there are a couple of simple things worth having ready before you open it for the first time:

  • A Windows computer with a modern web browser installed.
  • Your media services already running on your home network, reachable from the computer you are using.
  • The addresses and sign-in details for those services, kept somewhere handy so you can connect them.
  • A stable home network connection between your computer and your server.

If you are unsure of an address, it is usually the same link you already open in your browser when you visit that service directly.
